# Product vision

## The problem

Independent food-business owners often evaluate commercial spaces with incomplete information. A space that looks affordable can require substantial build-out, and delays before opening can add months of rent before the first sale. Generic listing tools describe properties; owners need to understand what a specific property would take to become *their* business.

## Product thesis

Help an owner understand what it would take to turn a specific commercial space into their restaurant before committing. Bring property and market context, interior spatial evidence, aerial/site context, and the operator's own requirements together to visualize possible operations, quantify financial implications, identify uncertainty, and recommend useful next steps.

The product is not simply a listing ranker, a virtual tour, a renovation image generator, or a revenue-prediction tool. Its distinctive promise is an evidence-grounded view of **what is there, what could fit, what it may cost, and what the owner should verify or negotiate**.

## Initial user and domain

Start with independent restaurants, cafes, and fast-casual businesses, particularly owners without a chain's data team. The initial inventory is limited to commercial properties with useful scans and verified sponsor data coverage, expected in Houston and Los Angeles. The owner may be opening a first location or expanding an existing operation; prior business layouts and costs can personalize the analysis when available and shared with permission.

## Signature experience

An owner describes their concept and constraints. For a candidate site, the product presents relevant property, aerial, and interior evidence. The owner can project a familiar kitchen/equipment arrangement into the candidate space, inspect fit and circulation, adjust the proposed plan, and see itemized cost and operational consequences update. Findings can lead to a verification checklist and possible landlord negotiation asks.

This is conceptual decision support, not an architectural drawing, inspection, code determination, or guarantee of business performance. It must distinguish observed facts from inference, estimates, and unknowns.

## Differentiation

- **Business-specific spatial projection:** visualize a real operator's kitchen and workflow in a different scanned space, not a generic floor-plan viewer.
- **Cross-layer reasoning:** combine property/market, interior, aerial/site, and business evidence to discover useful consequences that a single source cannot show. Build each data capability independently first; integrate deliberately and explainably.
- **Immediate cost-and-impact feedback:** recalculate a transparent build-out estimate as a plan changes. Cost is a quantification layer within the broader decision experience, not the whole product.
- **Site-to-operations scenarios:** compare feasible alternatives and show trade-offs among occupancy cost, build-out investment, capacity, access, and operational fit. Prefer Pareto-style alternatives over a single opaque “best” score.
- **Agentic diligence:** identify missing evidence, re-plan when a constraint changes the answer, and prepare owner-reviewed questions, negotiation asks, and decision materials.
- **Operator learning:** use an owner's own prior layout, cost history, and lessons as context. Future community benchmarks require consent, provenance, and careful normalization.

The intended sponsor-track “surprise” is a validated insight or action produced by combining data—not merely displaying several APIs on one page.

## Business and financial principles

Separate recurring occupancy costs, one-time build-out, and delay-related costs. Itemize the quantities, prices, assumptions, ranges, contingency, and unknowns behind an estimate. A benchmark such as occupancy cost as a share of sales is not break-even analysis. Any operating scenario requires explicit sales and expense assumptions and must be presented as a scenario, not a forecast or promise.

Design for independent operators who need practical decision support, not just chains with analytics teams. Validate the most important workflow with an actual local business owner; their input should shape scope, not merely serve as pitch evidence.
